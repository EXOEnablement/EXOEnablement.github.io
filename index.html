Onboarding · Stage by stage

Ramp properly. Not just quickly.

Day one sets the tone for everything after it.

The new SDR arrives to working tools, a named buddy, and a plan they can see — not a login hunt and a shrug. First impressions are a retention decision: this is where the experience is protected, or lost.

  • + Working kit, day one
  • + A plan they can see
  • + A person expecting them
The manager's job: be present

Week one is for craft foundations, not content dumps.

Shadowing real calls, the scorecard introduced early so quality is visible from the start, and first reps at outreach with feedback the same day — structured so a capable new hire can absorb a lot, in the order they will actually use it.

  • + Live call shadowing
  • + Scorecard from the start
  • + Feedback the same day
The manager's job: coach, don't broadcast

Month three is where ramp gets measured honestly.

Benchmarks, not vibes: time-to-first-milestone, activity-to-quota progression, early conversion curves, and 90-day survival. Where the curves bend is where coaching goes — one focus skill at a time, visible to the rep.

  • + Speed · time to first milestone
  • + Velocity · activity to quota
  • + Quality · early conversion curves
  • + Retention · 90-day survival
The manager's job: coach the bend in the curve

Promotion should be a readiness decision, not a vacancy decision.

SDRs ready to run discovery and own deals move up with their craft intact — assessed against the same standards they were coached on, so the team behind them can see exactly what good looks like and what to aim at.

  • + Discovery ownership
  • + Assessed, not assumed
  • + A visible standard for the team
The manager's job: promote on evidence

How I work

Style

Coaches from experience, having carried the number and used MEDDICC, SPICED, BANT and Challenger in live deals. Fits the method to your team and gives direct feedback, including the hard conversations. Frameworks: GROW, Kolb, 70:20:10 and Kirkpatrick.

Tools & AI

AI role-play creation and AI agents for prospecting. Scorecard design, sign-off assessment design. eLearning creation. Custom AI Coaching Model. Hands-on experience with Gong, Yoodli, Salesforce, HubSpot, Pipedrive, Outreach, Orum and Seismic.

The EXO view: protect the experience.

EXO is End-User Experience Orientation — a philosophy, not a playbook. Sales development is designed around the experience of the person on the other end of the call, and the person making it. Speed, activity and volume are easy to count. Trust is what compounds.

Hard on the problem. Invested in the person. AI as an amplifier, never a replacement — and the SDR role treated as a craft, not disposable entry-level labour.
